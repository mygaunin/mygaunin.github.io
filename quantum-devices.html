Design, Modelling, and Fabrication of High Frequency Oersted Lines for Electron Spin Manipulation

Conducted under Dr. Richard Silver at the Joint Quantum Institute (JQI) / NIST. Focused on developing robust microwave delivery infrastructure to manipulate single-dopant electron spins in silicon quantum devices.

COMSOL electromagnetic field simulation of microwave current distribution vs. scanning electron micrograph of fabricated Oersted lines.

The Engineering Challenge

Manipulating individual electron spins in silicon-based quantum computing architectures requires generating strong localized AC magnetic fields at microwave frequencies (up to 40 GHz) directly above atomic-scale donor structures. Traditional wire designs suffer from high impedance mismatch, excessive thermal dissipation in cryogenic environments, and uneven field distributions.

Methodology & Fabrication

  • Utilized COMSOL Multiphysics to model high-frequency electromagnetic field distributions and optimize line geometry.
  • Fabricated precise device structures on silicon chips utilizing advanced photolithography, metal deposition, and liftoff protocols at the NIST Nanofab center.
  • Integrated impedance matching networks to minimize signal reflection across the 40 GHz spectrum.

Results & Publication

This work successfully demonstrated reliable high-frequency electron spin resonance manipulation, paving the way for scalable donor-based quantum architectures. The findings were published in the Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology (JVST) B and recognized as an Editor's Pick.
