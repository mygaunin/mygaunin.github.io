High-Frequency Oersted Lines for Electron Spin Manipulation in Silicon Quantum Devices

Published in Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology (JVST) B · Special Collection: EIPBN

Reference Publication: DOI: 10.1116/6.0004051

Overview & Motivation

Coherent manipulation of electron spins is one of the central challenges in scaling silicon-based quantum computing architectures. Applying a static magnetic field (typically ~1.5 T) creates Zeeman energy splitting between spin-up and spin-down states. To drive coherent Rabi oscillations between these states, an oscillating electromagnetic field in the 10–60 GHz range (approximately 40 GHz for 1.5 T) must be delivered directly to the qubit structure operating inside millikelvin dilution refrigerators (<300 mK).

This project outlines a rigorous methodology for designing, modeling, fabricating, and testing coplanar waveguides (CPWs) and nanoscale electron spin resonance (ESR) / Oersted lines capable of transmitting high-frequency pulses with minimal insertion loss. Furthermore, we introduced a novel **Oersted-antenna design** that couples a symmetrical receiving antenna opposite the signal line to monitor electromagnetic field power directly at the device interface.

Figure 1: Three-dimensional model of an ESR line on a silicon chip surface inducing an oscillating magnetic field ($B$) onto a buried quantum dot device located ~30 nm below the surface.

COMSOL Multiphysics Modeling

To achieve impedance matching ($Z_0 \approx 50\ \Omega$) and prevent signal reflections across the test setup, finite element simulations were conducted using COMSOL Multiphysics. The model simulated the full test assembly consisting of:

  • A printed circuit board (PCB) fabricated on CLTE-AT Rogers dielectric material with gold-plated copper ground planes.
  • Gold wire bonds interfacing the PCB coplanar waveguides to the silicon chip CPW pads.
  • Transmission parameters ($S_{21}$) and reflection parameters ($S_{11}$) evaluated across the 34–46 GHz frequency span.

Simulations successfully identified standing wave behaviors and cavity resonance ripples spaced by ~2 GHz, corresponding to a cavity length of $L \approx 4.3\text{ cm}$ matching the distance from the PCB SMP connectors to the wire-bonded chip edge.

Cleanroom Fabrication & Test Infrastructure

Test structures and Oersted-antenna devices were fabricated at the NIST NanoFab using standard photolithography and lift-off techniques:

  1. Resist Stack: A bilayer resist comprising LOR 3A and S1813 was spun on undoped silicon wafers to ensure clean lift-off profiles.
  2. Patterning: Exposure via a direct laser writer at a dose of $90\text{ mJ/cm}^2$, followed by development in MF 319 and oxygen plasma descum.
  3. Metallization: E-beam evaporation of a 5 nm titanium adhesion layer followed by 150 nm of gold at an angle to prevent conformal sidewall coating.
  4. Lift-Off: Immersion in Remover PG at 80 °C, rinsed in isopropyl alcohol and de-ionized water.

The Oersted-antenna devices featured variable center gap spacings ranging from 0.5 µm to 2.0 µm. The 0.5 µm gap represented the minimum reliable feature size achievable via optical photolithography.

Key Results & Findings

Inversely Proportional Coupling

Measurements verified that magnetic field magnitude and transmitted power across the gap are inversely proportional to distance, showing optimal microwave transmittance for smaller 0.5 µm gap spacings.

Millitesla Field Generation

At the 42 GHz operating point, normalized transmittance data corresponded to induced magnetic fields on the order of several millitesla at the qubit interface, confirming sufficient power to drive high-fidelity Rabi oscillations.
